# Daily puzzle catalog

Today’s puzzle is selected by UTC date. The archive lists dates starting November 8, 2026. Every date has a permanent route and is playable without an account. Answers are stored on the current browser by date; the previous single-answer format is still readable. Continuing into the hand does not alter the main practice save.

The catalog is procedural: a date selects a recipe version, a seed and a fixed card pattern. The launch puzzle (November 8) uses the Training Card. November 9 onward uses the 55 printed 2025 Card lines in card order (v2), with the same card used when continuing the game. The v2 answer is outside every playable variant of the printed line, so its suit and rank options do not create an equally good discard. The earlier November 9 Training Card answer does not count toward the new puzzle. The generator allocates a legal winning hand, removes a natural tile, adds one nonmatching tile, and deals the remaining physical tiles to the wall and bots. The needed natural tile stays in the wall. There are no nightly jobs or database records. The catalog reconstructs earlier racks, including their original tile order.

Keep the v1 rotation, training pattern definitions, deck order and shuffle stable for published dates. A new generation method should use a new recipe version from a specified date, or add authored entries with an explicit date and fixture. The original launch puzzle is covered by a regression test.

## Table-reading puzzles

“Would you throw it?” is a separate three-level collection in the archive. Each authored snapshot uses physical tiles from one deck. The player sees their own rack, opposing exposures and public discards. The puzzle counts printed 2025 Card lines once per opponent, after testing variants against the exposed sets and unavailable tiles. A lower count is the puzzle objective, not a prediction that a discard is safe.

- Easy compares two choices against one exposed set.
- Medium adds a second exposure and visible discards that eliminate a line.
- Hard weighs different lines across two opponents.

Players can retry and earn one star per level. Stars are saved by level on the device. The feedback highlights the clues and shows the actual card rows used in the comparison. Concealed opponent tiles are never used to grade an answer.
